Ember privacy policy

Effective: 25 October 2026

Ember is a step counter for Apple Watch with an iPhone app and iPhone widgets. It is made by Nishanth Suresh, an individual developer in the Netherlands. This policy explains what Ember does with your data, and what happens if you contact me.

The short version

Ember does not collect your data. It has no account, no servers, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. Your step history stays on your Apple Watch and your iPhone, and in your own backups of them. Ember never sends it to its developer or to anyone else.

What Ember reads

On your Apple Watch, with your permission, Ember reads these from Apple Health:

  • Steps
  • Walking and running distance
  • Active energy
  • Flights climbed
  • Exercise minutes

On your iPhone, with your permission, Ember reads your steps from Apple Health. It reads today’s steps and the daily totals of up to 400 days before today, so your history can start before you installed Ember. The iPhone widgets read today’s steps under the same permission.

With your permission, Ember uses Motion & Fitness on your watch to count your steps live while the app is open and for the watch face complication.

Ember uses this data to show your progress and to suggest daily goals. It appears in the watch app, its complications, the iPhone app, its widgets and the optional reminder.

What Ember writes to Health

Nothing. Ember only reads from Apple Health and never writes to it.

What Ember stores

On your Apple Watch, Ember stores your daily step totals, the goal in force on each day, a snapshot of today’s steps by hour, the goal days and step milestones you reached, your usual walking times (with Ember Pro), your walk time and reminder setting, your theme, whether setup is done, whether Ember Pro is unlocked, and a short Diagnostics log of recent background updates for troubleshooting.

On your iPhone, Ember stores its own step history and goals, a copy of the latest data from your watch, today’s steps and the last seven days for the widgets, your theme, whether setup is done, whether Ember Pro is unlocked, and whether Ember has asked you to rate it. It works without an Apple Watch.

Both devices store your weekly targets, your choice to show or hide streaks, and the times you changed those settings so they can sync correctly.

Ember keeps your step history until you delete Ember from both devices.

Between your devices

Ember exchanges step history, daily and weekly goals, streak visibility, edit times and reading timestamps between your own watch and iPhone. The watch also sends whether setup is done and whether Pro is unlocked. Pro access is verified separately with the App Store. This travels only between your own two devices, through Apple’s Watch Connectivity, encrypted by Apple.

Backups and iCloud

Ember does not use iCloud. Your Apple Watch backs up to your iPhone. If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or to a computer, that backup can include Ember’s data, like any app’s data. Apple encrypts iCloud backups. With Advanced Data Protection turned on, only your devices hold the keys. You can leave Ember out of your iPhone’s iCloud Backup in Settings › [your name] › iCloud › iCloud Backup.

What Ember shares

Nothing. Ember does not sell, share or transfer your data to anyone, and it does not use Health or motion data for advertising or marketing.

Ember has no way to send anything to another person. There is no account, no contacts list and no sharing button.

The reminder

The daily reminder is off until you turn it on. If you turn it on, your watch schedules one notification a day at your walk time, on the watch itself. It can show how many steps are left to your goal. Nothing comes from a server.

Ember Pro and purchases

Ember Pro, which includes the extra themes, is a one-time purchase through the App Store. Apple sells it and processes the payment under Apple’s own terms and privacy policy. In the EU the seller is Apple Distribution International Ltd. Refunds and the 14-day right of withdrawal are handled by Apple. Ember keeps only whether Pro is unlocked. Apple gives me sales reports, which do not include your name or payment details.

Crash reports and statistics from Apple

Ember contains no analytics or crash reporting code. If you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in your device’s settings, Apple may pass crash reports and statistics about Ember on to me. Apple says it shares these only in aggregated form or in a form that does not identify you. You can turn this off in Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements › Share With App Developers.

If you email me

If you write to the support address, I receive your email address, your name if you include it, and what you write. I use this only to answer you and to fix problems you report. I never use it for marketing. My legal basis is my legitimate interest in answering questions about Ember (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). I delete support emails within a year after our conversation ends, unless I need one longer for a legal claim.

The support address is a Gmail account, so Google stores these emails for me, possibly on servers in the United States.

You can object to this use of your emails at any time. Write to me and I will delete them, unless I need one for a legal claim.

This website

This page and the support page are hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub logs the IP address of every visitor for security purposes, under GitHub’s own privacy statement. I do not receive these logs. The pages use no cookies, analytics or trackers.

Health data and your consent

Everything Ember reads from Apple Health and Motion & Fitness, and everything it stores, stays on your devices and in your own backups of them. I cannot see it and never receive it. If the law treats me as responsible for this processing anyway, it rests on your explicit consent, which you give in Apple’s permission screens (Article 9(2)(a) and Article 6(1)(a) GDPR). You can withdraw it at any time with the switches described below. Withdrawing does not undo what Ember showed you before. Giving access is your choice. Ember needs Health or Motion access to count your steps. If you withdraw both, it stops counting new steps.

Your choices

  • Health access: you can turn Ember’s access to each Health category on or off at any time in the Health app on your iPhone. Tap your profile picture, then Apps, then Ember.
  • Motion access: you can turn it off on your watch in Settings › Privacy & Security › Motion & Fitness › Ember.
  • The reminder: you can turn it off in Ember on your watch, in Settings › Walk time.
  • Delete your data: deleting Ember from a device removes what Ember stored on that device. To remove it everywhere, delete Ember from both your Apple Watch and your iPhone. Older backups keep a copy until they are replaced or deleted. Your steps stay in Apple Health, where you can delete them.

Your rights

You can ask me for access to, correction of or deletion of personal data I hold about you, ask me to restrict or stop using it, and ask for a copy in a portable form. For data on your devices, you already hold it: you can see it in Ember and delete it by deleting the app. For support emails, write to the address below. I answer within one month.

You can also complain to the Dutch data protection authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl), or to the authority in the EU country where you live or work.

Automated decisions

Ember suggests goals based on your recent steps. This happens on your device, has no legal or similar effect on you, and you decide whether to accept a suggestion. Ember makes no other automated decisions.

Children

Ember is a general step counter and is not aimed at children. The app collects no personal information from anyone. If a child emails me, I use the email only to reply and delete it as described above.
