Ember privacy policy

Effective: 25 October 2026

Ember is a step counter for Apple Watch with an iPhone app and iPhone widgets, made by Nishanth Suresh. This policy explains what Ember does with your data.

The short version

Ember does not collect your data. It has no account, no servers, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. Your step history stays on your Apple Watch and your iPhone, and in your own backups of them. Ember never sends it to its developer or to anyone else.

What Ember reads

On your Apple Watch, with your permission, Ember reads these from Apple Health:

  • Steps
  • Walking and running distance
  • Active energy
  • Flights climbed
  • Exercise minutes

On your iPhone, with your permission, Ember reads your steps from Apple Health. It reads today’s steps and the daily totals of up to 400 days before today, so your history can start before you installed Ember. The iPhone widgets read today’s steps under the same permission.

With your permission, Ember uses Motion & Fitness on your watch to count your steps live while the app is open and for the watch face complication.

Ember uses this data to show your progress and to suggest an initial daily goal. It appears in the watch app, its complications, the iPhone app, its widgets and the optional reminder.

What Ember writes to Health

Nothing. Ember only reads from Apple Health and never writes to it.

What Ember stores

On your Apple Watch, Ember stores your daily step totals, the goal in force on each day, a snapshot of today’s steps by hour, the goal days and step milestones you reached, your usual walking times (with Ember Pro), your walk time and reminder setting, your theme, whether setup is done, whether Ember Pro is unlocked, and a short Diagnostics log of recent background updates for troubleshooting.

On your iPhone, Ember stores its own step history and goals, today’s steps and the last seven days for the widgets, your theme, and whether Ember Pro is unlocked. It works without an Apple Watch.

Both devices store your weekly targets, your choice to show or hide streaks, and the times you changed those settings so they can sync correctly.

Ember keeps your step history until you delete Ember from both devices.

Between your devices

Ember exchanges step history, daily and weekly goals, streak visibility, edit times and reading timestamps between your own watch and iPhone. The watch also sends whether setup is done and Pro is unlocked; Pro access is verified separately with the App Store. This travels only between your own two devices, through Apple’s Watch Connectivity, encrypted by Apple.

Backups and iCloud

Ember does not use iCloud. Your Apple Watch backs up to your iPhone. If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or to a computer, that backup can include Ember’s data, like any app’s data. Apple encrypts iCloud backups. With Advanced Data Protection turned on, only your devices hold the keys. You can leave Ember out of your iPhone’s iCloud Backup in Settings › [your name] › iCloud › iCloud Backup.

What Ember shares

Nothing. Ember does not sell, share or transfer your data to anyone, and it does not use Health or motion data for advertising or marketing.

Ember has no way to send anything to another person. There is no account, no contacts list and no sharing button.

The reminder

The daily reminder is off until you turn it on. If you turn it on, your watch schedules one notification a day at your walk time, on the watch itself. It can show how many steps are left to your goal. Nothing comes from a server.

Ember Pro and themes

Ember Pro, which includes the extra themes, is a one-time purchase through the App Store. Apple processes the payment. Ember learns only whether Pro is unlocked.

Crash reports

Ember contains no analytics or crash reporting code. If you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in your device’s settings, Apple may pass crash reports about Ember on to its developer.

Your choices

  • Health access: you can turn Ember’s access to each Health category on or off at any time in the Health app on your iPhone. Tap your profile picture, then Apps, then Ember.
  • Motion access: you can turn it off on your watch in Settings › Privacy & Security › Motion & Fitness › Ember.
  • The reminder: you can turn it off in Ember on your watch, in Settings › Walk time.
  • Delete your data: deleting Ember from a device removes what Ember stored on that device. To remove it everywhere, delete Ember from both your Apple Watch and your iPhone. Older backups keep a copy until they are replaced or deleted. Your steps stay in Apple Health, where you can delete them.

Children

Ember does not knowingly collect any personal information, from children or anyone else.

Changes
